# NOBS Customer Discovery Protocol

**Purpose:** Produce decision-grade evidence for the NOBS daily-planning wedge without leading participants or overstating the prototype.

## Participant profile

Recruit adults who use an iPhone and personally manage a busy mix of work and life commitments. Seek variation across job type, caregiving responsibilities, planning style, and accessibility needs. Do not diagnose or recruit people based on inferred health conditions.

Keep a screening record with only the minimum information needed:

- iPhone user;
- manages at least two planning sources, such as calendar, tasks, messages, or notes;
- experienced an overloaded or disrupted day in the last two weeks;
- willing to discuss current behavior;
- optional interest in a consented prototype trial.

## Research sequence

### 1. Problem interview — 30 minutes

Do not show NOBS until the behavioral questions are complete.

1. Tell me about the last day that felt impossible to organize.
2. What information did you check, and in what order?
3. Where did the plan break down?
4. What did you forget, delay, or renegotiate?
5. What did that cost you in time, stress, money, or relationships?
6. What have you tried to fix this?
7. Which tools do you still use? Which did you abandon, and why?
8. When would you trust software to suggest changes to your day?
9. What information would you refuse to share or send to a cloud service?
10. Have you paid for a planning, productivity, or AI tool in the last year? What caused that decision?

Finish by summarizing the participant's problem in your own words and ask what you misunderstood.

### 2. Prototype task — 30 minutes

State clearly which data is real, representative, or simulated and which actions are not yet implemented.

Prompt:

> Imagine this is tomorrow morning. Use NOBS to understand whether the day is realistic and decide what you would change. Please think aloud; I am testing the product, not you.

Observe without coaching:

- whether the participant understands the proposed priorities;
- whether conflicts and capacity issues feel accurate;
- which explanation creates or reduces trust;
- whether the processing label and privacy receipt are understood;
- which proposed action they accept, edit, or reject;
- what they expect to happen next;
- moments where the prototype implies capability it does not have.

### 3. Exit interview — 15 minutes

1. What was the most useful part?
2. What felt wrong, intrusive, or untrustworthy?
3. What would have to be true for you to use this three mornings next week?
4. What would you use instead if NOBS did not exist?
5. How would you explain NOBS to a friend?
6. At which price would this feel suspiciously cheap, like a good value, expensive but worth considering, and too expensive?
7. Would you choose a paid 30-day pilot, a refundable early-access deposit, a waitlist, or none? Why?
8. How disappointed would you be if this planning experience disappeared: not disappointed, somewhat disappointed, or very disappointed?

## Evidence standards

- Separate direct quotations, observed behavior, and researcher interpretation.
- Obtain explicit permission before recording or using a quotation.
- Remove names, employers, event titles, message content, and other identifying context from shared findings.
- Do not store participant calendar or message content in the repository.
- Record negative evidence and disconfirming cases, not only enthusiasm.
- A compliment is not demand. Return use, a real commitment, or a payment decision is stronger evidence.

## Weekly synthesis

Publish counts and themes, not private raw notes:

- interviews completed;
- qualified trials started;
- participants completing 1, 2, and 3 planning days;
- plan acceptance and edit rate;
- top three recurring problems;
- top three trust failures;
- pricing commitments;
- strongest disconfirming evidence;
- product or positioning decision made;
- next hypothesis to test.

Do not call the result “validated” merely because interviews were completed. Validation requires behavior consistent with the proof thresholds in [`FUNDING_STRATEGY.md`](FUNDING_STRATEGY.md).
