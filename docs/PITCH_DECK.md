## Slide 1 — NOBS

**Your technology. Finally working for you.**

NOBS turns a chaotic day into a realistic plan—privately, across the technology people already own.

**Visual:** One clean iPhone frame showing an overloaded-day diagnosis and a proposed fix.

## Slide 2 — The day is spread across inboxes

Working adults do not lack calendars, task lists, messages, and AI chatbots. They lack a system that understands the whole day well enough to say:

> This plan will not work. Here is why, and here is a safer version.

**Evidence required:** Three anonymized user observations showing the consequence of fragmented planning. Do not use generic productivity statistics as a substitute for direct problem evidence.

## Slide 3 — NOBS makes the day feasible

1. Reads approved calendar, task, and priority context.
2. Finds conflicts, missing transition time, and unrealistic capacity.
3. Produces a prioritized plan with an explanation.
4. Offers reversible fixes under the user's control.

**Visual:** Before/after daily timeline with the reasoning visible.

## Slide 4 — A private personal-intelligence layer

NOBS routes each job through the safest capable option:

- **Local:** private everyday assistance on the iPhone;
- **Tank:** heavier work on hardware the user owns;
- **NOBScloud:** optional capacity when local hardware needs help.

Every response identifies its route. Privacy receipts explain what information was used and where it was processed.

**Visual:** The simple Local → Tank → optional NOBScloud architecture already approved in the PRD.

## Slide 5 — Why this starts now

- On-device intelligence can finally support useful personal workflows.
- AI adoption creates demand for assistance but heightens the trust problem around personal context.
- Apple users already have rich context, fragmented across system services and apps.
- NOBS can begin with one high-frequency workflow and expand from earned trust.

**Evidence required:** Current primary-source support for platform capability and credible market research for customer behavior. Date every external data point.

**Market context:** The current [bottom-up U.S. market model](MARKET_SIZE.md) produces a $1.8B–$9.1B serviceable annual revenue range and a $4.6B rounded base case. Present it as a scenario, not validated TAM, until direct research replaces the problem-incidence and pricing assumptions.

## Slide 7 — Beachhead to platform

**Beachhead:** daily planning for overwhelmed working adults.

**Expansion sequence:**

1. communication and commitments;
2. private memory and research;
3. household intelligence and trusted automations;
4. cross-platform smart-home unification;
5. skills, hosted Tank, and future NOBSbox.

The wedge is narrow enough to ship. The trust and routing layer is broad enough to compound.

## Slide 8 — Business model without surveillance

Local private chat, basic daily planning, privacy controls, and user-owned Tank support remain free.

Revenue comes from optional capability:

- NOBScloud subscription;
- advanced local and Tank features;
- hosted Tank and family plans;
- reviewed paid skills;
- future NOBSbox hardware.

**Evidence required:** Tested price points, willingness-to-pay commitments, initial cloud unit-cost model, and a clear first paid package.

## Slide 9 — Why NOBS can win

- An Apple-native experience centered on a repeated daily job;
- transparent policy-controlled routing across local, user-owned, and cloud compute;
- user-approved memory and accumulated preferences;
- trust infrastructure for permissions, automations, privacy receipts, and skill safety;
- expansion into the household after earning individual trust.

Call this a **defensibility thesis**, not a moat, until return use and switching value are observed.

## Sixty-second spoken version

Most working adults already have calendars, task lists, messages, and AI chatbots. What they do not have is a system that can look at the whole day and say, “This plan is impossible—here is a realistic version, and here is why.” NOBS is building that system for the Apple ecosystem. It runs locally whenever possible, can use hardware the customer owns for heavier work, and uses NOBScloud only when permitted. Every action stays visible and reversible. We are starting with the overloaded-day planning loop, validating repeated use and willingness to pay, then expanding into communication, household intelligence, and trusted automation. We are raising [amount] to turn the current working prototype into a validated iOS beta and prove that private personal intelligence can become a durable consumer platform.
