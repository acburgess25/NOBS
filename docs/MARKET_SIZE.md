# NOBS Market Size

**Status:** Working bottom-up estimate for investor diligence  
**Updated:** July 2, 2026  
**Geography:** United States launch market  
**Measurement:** Potential annual subscription revenue, not company valuation

## Answer first

The current bottom-up model estimates a **$1.8B–$9.1B U.S. serviceable annual revenue pool**, with a **$4.6B base case**, for a paid daily-planning product aimed at overwhelmed working adults who use iPhones.

This range is intentionally broad. Only the employed population and smartphone-ownership inputs are strong external evidence. iPhone share is a usage-share proxy, while problem prevalence, paid conversion, and price are hypotheses that NOBS must validate directly.

For fundraising, the more useful near-term claim is not “NOBS owns a multibillion-dollar market.” It is:

> A credible U.S. beachhead can support a venture-scale company if NOBS proves repeated use and paid demand in a narrow cohort.

## Market definition

NOBS begins with overwhelmed working adults in the United States who:

1. use an iPhone;
2. manage work and personal commitments across multiple planning sources;
3. experience recurring planning overload;
4. are willing to pay for a product that turns those inputs into a realistic daily plan.

This estimate excludes smart-home revenue, NOBSbox hardware, paid skills, family plans, business plans, hosted Tank, and international expansion. Those are expansion opportunities, not part of the launch-market calculation.

## Sizing method

The model is bottom-up:

```text
U.S. employed people
× smartphone ownership
× iOS usage-share proxy
× recurring planning-overload incidence
× annual paid price
= serviceable annual revenue pool
```

This approach is preferable to quoting the total productivity-software or AI-assistant category because it stays tied to the launch user, platform, problem, and business model.

### Pricing context

- Sunsama publishes $17 per month billed annually and $22 billed monthly for its daily-planning product.
- Reclaim publishes a $12 per-seat monthly Starter plan alongside a free tier.

Those products are not exact substitutes for NOBS, and their prices do not prove NOBS willingness to pay. They support testing **$10, $15, and $20 monthly equivalents** rather than assuming the product must be priced like a basic task list.

## Calculation

| Scenario | Employed smartphone users on iOS proxy | Users with recurring overload | Annual price | Serviceable annual revenue pool |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Low | 73.0M | 14.6M | $120 | **$1.75B** |
| Base | 84.7M | 25.4M | $180 | **$4.57B** |
| High | 94.9M | 38.0M | $240 | **$9.11B** |

Base-case arithmetic:

```text
162.264M × 90% × 58% × 30% × $180 = $4.57B annual revenue pool
```

Rounding is deliberate; input precision does not justify a more precise output.

## TAM, SAM, and SOM interpretation

### TAM — broader platform context

Apple reported more than **2.5 billion active devices** in January 2026. This validates the scale of the ecosystem but is not a usable customer count: one person may own several devices, and the figure spans products and geographies. Do not multiply 2.5 billion devices by a subscription price.

The eventual global personal-intelligence TAM is therefore left unquantified until NOBS has a defensible user denominator, geographic rollout assumptions, and observed pricing.

### SAM — U.S. launch revenue pool

The $1.8B–$9.1B range above is the serviceable annual revenue pool for the current launch definition. The $4.6B base case is suitable as a transparent planning midpoint, not as a forecast.

### SOM — five-year operating scenarios

SOM should be expressed as an execution scenario rather than a top-down market-share claim:

| Paid users | Annual revenue at $180 | Share of base-case overload segment |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 25,000 | $4.5M | 0.10% |
| 100,000 | $18.0M | 0.39% |
| 250,000 | $45.0M | 0.98% |

These are scale markers, not projections. A real five-year forecast requires acquisition channels, activation, conversion, retention, churn, gross margin, and model-cost evidence.

## Sensitivity

The estimate is most sensitive to three unvalidated inputs:

1. **Problem incidence:** moving from 20% to 40% doubles the pool.
2. **Price:** moving from $10 to $20 per month doubles the pool.
3. **Paid behavior:** the model describes a potential revenue pool, not the percentage NOBS will convert.

iOS share matters, but less than proving that planning overload is frequent, painful, and payable.

## What would make this investor-ready

Before using the base case as a deck headline:

- complete at least 15 launch-persona interviews;
- measure how many qualified participants report the problem weekly or more often;
- test $10, $15, and $20 monthly equivalents using real commitment choices;
- measure trial activation and return use;
- distinguish individual-paid, employer-expensed, and family-paid demand;
- build a bottom-up acquisition model for the first 1,000 and 10,000 users.

The direct research should replace the 20%–40% incidence assumption and narrow the price range. Until then, the deck should label the market figure **“bottom-up scenario”** rather than “validated TAM.”
